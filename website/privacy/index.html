Albus

Privacy policy

Last updated 1 October 2026

Albus is an iPhone app that plans study time. This page explains what information it handles, why, who helps us run it, and the choices you have.

The short version

  • No name, email address or phone number is required to use Albus. Your account is anonymous; a display name is optional.
  • Albus stores your assignments, plans, saved rubrics and marking results to provide the app's features.
  • When you ask for an AI plan or for marking, the text that request needs is sent to Anthropic, which runs the AI. Anthropic does not use API content for model training by default under its commercial terms.
  • We don't save the full submission in our database. Saved feedback can include excerpts from your work.
  • A security check from Cloudflare runs while you set up Albus, to stop programs creating accounts in bulk.
  • No ads, no advertising identifier and no tracking across apps or websites.
  • Apple handles payments. We never see your card or your Apple ID.

1. Who is responsible

Albus is published by the developer named as the seller on its App Store page ("we", "us"). We are the controller of the personal data described here. Contact: fgutort8@gmail.com.

2. What we collect, and why

Your account

When you finish setting up Albus, it creates an anonymous account with our database provider, Supabase. The account has a random identifier and no name, email address or password. It lets your plans sync to our server and lets us apply your plan's limits. Why: to provide the app you asked for.

What you put into Albus

  • Your assignments: titles, deadlines, how long you expect them to take, the kind of work, and the steps Albus plans for them.
  • Your study preferences, such as the hours you like to study and how much time you have each day, and a display name if you add one.
  • The subjects you add, and the rubrics and mark schemes you save.
  • How long a study step actually took and at what hour of the day. These records contain no text and are used to make Albus's time estimates more accurate.

Why: to provide the app you asked for.

AI plans and marking

  • For an AI plan, Albus sends the assignment's details, such as its title, the kind of work, its deadline and the time you expect it to take, to Anthropic, which writes the steps.
  • For marking, Albus first turns your file or photo into text on your phone. The extracted text, relevant title and instructions, and the rubric you chose are sent to Anthropic. The file or photo itself never leaves your phone.
  • We store the mark and the feedback, and a fingerprint (a one-way hash) of the text, which lets us recognise the same work sent twice. We do not save the full submission in our database. Saved feedback can quote your work, though: each criterion can include a quotation of up to 400 characters, and the written comments and feedback can repeat or paraphrase other parts of it. It also keeps your work's title. Our AI provider also processes the submission under its own retention terms.
  • For every AI request we record its kind, the AI model used, its size in tokens and what it cost. These records contain none of your work. We use them to apply your plan's limits and to keep the cost of running Albus under control.

Why: to provide the feature you asked for.

Subscriptions

If you subscribe, Apple takes the payment. RevenueCat checks the purchase with Apple and tells our server which plan your account has. We store the subscription's identifiers, product, dates and status, and an estimate of what we received after tax and Apple's commission, which we use to set how much Albus may spend on AI. We never receive your name, your Apple ID or your payment details. Each purchase, renewal or refund RevenueCat reports is first saved in a queue, with your account's random identifier and the purchase details above, so that none is lost or counted twice, and then applied. Every change to plans and payment records is also written to a log that can't be edited, which identifies accounts only by a one-way code. Why: to provide what you paid for, and to keep the accounting records the law requires.

Keeping Albus fair and safe

To stop people creating many accounts to get around the free limits, Albus sends an identifier that Apple gives to apps from the same developer (not an advertising identifier). Our server also sees your IP address when the app connects. In our abuse-detection tables, the IP address is shortened to its network part, and both values are replaced with a keyed one-way code. These tables do not store the raw identifier or IP address. Our hosting and authentication providers may separately keep network information in operational and security logs. We also keep short security records, such as which feature was used and an error code, never anything you wrote. Why: our legitimate interest in preventing abuse and keeping Albus affordable, including free.

When you set up Albus, it also runs a check from Cloudflare, called Turnstile, that tells people apart from programs creating accounts in bulk. It runs in the background while you answer the setup questions, and asks you to tick a box only if it isn't sure. It can run more than once during set-up: a pass is only good for a few minutes, so a new check runs if set-up takes longer, if a check stalls, or if creating the account fails. To decide, Cloudflare processes signals such as your IP address and technical details of your phone's connection and software, like its iOS version. Cloudflare also uses these signals to improve its bot detection, as its Turnstile privacy notice describes. Our server receives only a one-time pass that says the check succeeded. The check runs in a private web view that keeps nothing on your phone afterwards. Why: our legitimate interest in preventing abuse.

Reminders

Study reminders are scheduled on your phone. Albus does not use a push notification service.

On your phone

Albus keeps a copy of your plans on your iPhone so it works offline.

3. What we don't do

We don't show ads. We don't sell or share personal data for advertising. We don't use third-party analytics for study activity, and we don't track you across other apps or websites. RevenueCat processes subscription activity as described above.

5. How long we keep it

  • Your account and what you put into Albus: until you delete your account. An account that was never used (set-up not finished, nothing saved and no activity for 30 days) is removed automatically.
  • The Cloudflare check: our server keeps nothing from it. Cloudflare keeps its signals as its Turnstile privacy notice describes.
  • Records of AI requests that failed or were abandoned: 30 days. Records of completed requests, which contain no content, are kept for cost accounting.
  • Rate-limit counters: about two hours.
  • The one-way codes made from device identifiers and IP addresses: removed by scheduled cleanup after 90 days without another observation. Security records: 180 days.
  • Subscription records, and the log of changes to plans and payments: as long as accounting and tax law require.
  • Payment events in the queue: their details for 30 days after they are applied; after that, only an identifier, a fingerprint and the outcome, which stop the same event being applied twice. An event that hasn't been applied yet, including one that failed and is waiting for us to sort it out by hand, keeps its details until it is applied, so the payment isn't lost; the 30 days start then. An account with a payment still waiting in the queue is never removed as unused.

6. Your choices and rights

  • Delete your account in the app: Settings → Delete account. This removes your account, student content on our server and the local cache on this phone. Financial and security records described above survive under their existing retention rules; financial records no longer link to your account. Deleting your account does not cancel a subscription; cancel it first in your iPhone's Settings → your name → Subscriptions.
  • Remove Albus from your phone by deleting the app, which removes the copy stored there.
  • You can ask us for a copy of your data, to correct it, to delete it, to restrict how we use it, or object to how we use it, by writing to fgutort8@gmail.com.
  • If you think we have handled your data wrongly, you can complain to the data protection authority where you live. In Spain, that is the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos.

7. Children

Albus is made for students, including teenagers. It asks for no contact details, shows no ads and does no tracking. You must be 13 or older to use it. If you are under 16, a parent or guardian must read this policy and agree to the terms of service for you.

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. If a change matters, we will also say so in the app.
